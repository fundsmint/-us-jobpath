REMOTE WORK

How to check if a US remote job accepts international applicants

A “remote” job is not necessarily open to applicants worldwide. Some employers allow remote work only within a particular US state or country because of payroll, tax, data-security, or legal requirements.

Read the location wording

Look for phrases such as “US residents only,” “must be authorized to work in the United States,” “remote within [state],” or “eligible to work from any country.” If the wording is unclear, ask the employer's official recruitment contact before spending time on an application.

Check work authorization requirements

For work physically performed in the United States, you generally need appropriate work authorization. Working remotely from another country is a different situation and may still be restricted by the employer's policies and local rules.

Look for transparent employers

Use the employer's official career page, verify the recruiter’s email domain, and check the job title against the original posting. Never pay a recruiter for a guaranteed job offer.

Before you apply

  • Confirm the role is still open.
  • Confirm the permitted work location.
  • Check required skills, time zone, equipment, and working hours.
  • Ask whether international applicants are considered if the listing is unclear.
